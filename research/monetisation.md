## 1. Bottom line

1. **No public Taiwan rate data by follower tier was found, and the one industry view I did find says there may be none to find.** A Taiwan VTuber agency told Mirror Media (2024) that corporate collaborations are treated as marketing budgets, not a fixed per-post rate, and no public 業配 rate card turned up [S7]. The Phase 8 rate card cannot be built from public data. It needs at least three quotes from agencies or comparable creators (section 4).
2. **Evidence on what a disclosed AI persona earns from followers is weak and discouraging.** Engagement on first-generation virtual influencers is low (Imma 0.2% to 0.3%, Rozy 1.9%, Lil Miquela's BMW posts 0.6% versus 3.6% for BMW's human creators) [S3][S4][S5]. Most Taiwan indie VTubers earn "a few thousand NT$ a month" [S8]. The only large figure is a self-reported average of €3,000 a month for Aitana López from her agency's founder (2023), which is one outlier with no audit [S6].
3. **Platform payouts will not contribute in year 1.** YouTube's entry bar is 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 watch hours or 10 million Shorts views, and for new applicants from 2027-02-01 it is reported to double [S17]. TikTok Creator Rewards availability in Taiwan: not found [S20]. Instagram, Threads and Facebook payout programmes in Taiwan: not found.
4. **The strongest conversion lever is the one a persona may not use.** 52.5% of social users, and 60.3% of 25-34s, say others' shared experience (體驗文) makes them want to try a product [S10]; Ground rule 4 bars invented experience. Combined with Kolr (iKala) 2026 framing verifiable human creators as the scarce trust asset [S2], I expect both 業配 and affiliate conversion to trail human creators. This is inference, not a measured Taiwan result.
5. **The best-fitting early route is probably B2B: AI ad creative sold by the studio, not follower-based income.** Taiwan brands are making public mistakes with undisclosed or sloppy AI imagery (Life8 likeness dispute, IKEA Taiwan's distorted hands) [S31][S32]; the Digital Industry Administration (數位產業署) reportedly fined Meta NT$5 million for undisclosed AI-person ads (press reports dated 2026-09-23; the decision date and statute article numbers are unverified) [S13]; and the operator has fashion contacts. Price per job: not found. Capacity: a handful of jobs a year at 6 h/week.
6. **LINE is a big surface but a thin revenue line.** Taiwan users send about 60 million stickers a day [S22], and the Creators Market terms do not ban AI-made stickers but let LINE label them [S21]. Revenue share is only recalled, not verified [U1], and the payout threshold is not found. LINE OA costs rise on 2026-11-01 (中用量 NT$800 to NT$1,000; 高用量 NT$1,200 to NT$1,400, ex-tax) and OA earns nothing by itself [S24].
7. **At the NT$5,000 cap the studio is unlikely to be cash-positive in year 1.** Twelve months at the cap is NT$60,000 of spend. My hypothetical mid scenario brings in about NT$38,000, the low scenario about NT$1,000, and only a high scenario with real B2B traction (about NT$202,000) clears the cap (section 6). These are assumption-driven estimates, not forecasts.
8. **Every route where a brand pays needs the registered business.** B2B, 業配 and licensing all end in an invoice or contract; the operator's own note says registration is needed "before invoicing brands" [DECISIONS.md Q5]. Whether affiliate payouts, stickers and product sales can run before registration is unverified and belongs to the 記帳士 (section 8).
9. **Paid collaboration and the AI-person advertising duty interlock.** If a brand boosts a persona post as a partnership ad, the Fraud Crime Hazard Prevention Act (詐欺犯罪危害防制條例) duty to disclose AI-generated personal images in ads applies, and Meta has just been fined for failing it [S13][S14]. Contracts and workflow must set the platform's AI-disclosure control on every boosted post.

## 3. Route detail

### 3.1 Sponsored posts 業配, including per-video, CPM and CPC

**Market context (verified second-hand)**
- AnyMind's 2026 APAC report puts Taiwan's influencer-marketing market at about US$239 million, up 11.7% a year, with Instagram carrying 57.7% of campaigns, usually paired with Facebook and YouTube [S1].
- Kolr (iKala) 2026 frames verifiable human creators as the asset brands now want [S2]. For a disclosed AI persona this is a headwind, not a ban.
- NielsenIQ (a vendor study, Dec 2024) found AI-made ads rated more annoying, boring and confusing; a Hootsuite 2026 figure, quoted second-hand, says one third of consumers say AI-made brand ads lower purchase intent [S33][S34].

**Rates by follower tier (Taiwan)**

I deliberately left this empty. Foreign rate cards and unsourced blog ranges are not substitutes and would anchor Phase 8 on numbers nobody can defend. Every persona in its first year is nano or micro by any common definition (assumption), so the nano and micro rows are the ones that matter most.

**Related signals I did find**
- Corporate collaborations are budgeted case by case, not from a rate card (Taiwan VTuber agency, 2024) [S7]. Springfish says it has done 280+ cross-industry collaborations, but no fees are public [S29].
- For Taiwan VTubers the stated income order is donations (抖內) and memberships first, then merch, with 業配 last [S7]. Japan's ANYCOLOR mix is cited as merch 44%, live-stream income 31%, advertising and sponsorship 14% (low confidence, second-hand) [S9]. Advertising is a minority line even for the biggest VTuber business, and a 6 h/week solo studio cannot run live streams.
- Virtual-influencer sponsored-post performance is weak: Lil Miquela's BMW campaign averaged 0.6% engagement versus 3.6% for BMW's human creators (CreatorIQ, window May 2023 to Apr 2024) [S3]; Imma 0.2% to 0.3% [S4]; Rozy 1.9% [S5]. Rozy's two Shinhan Life TV spots reached 17.2 million combined views, which shows brands do buy campaign assets, in Korea and from a corporate sponsor [S5].

**Requirements**
- A brand willing to sign. Brands must tag the partner with Meta's branded-content tool and give permission; Meta counts gifts and free trips as "value", so a product-for-post deal still needs the label [S15].
- Some partner categories (dating, gambling, pharmacies and prescription drugs, crypto) need Meta's written pre-authorisation [S15]. They are outside Ground rule 6 anyway.
- A native reviewer on every sponsored post (BRIEF; DECISIONS Q3).

**Fit and sequencing**
- Capacity: a sponsored post is one post but several approval rounds (brand, native reviewer, operator, label check). With about 42 posts a week as the platforms.md upper bound before rework, a few sponsored posts a month is feasible; many are not.
- **Briefs will often ask for 體驗文.** Because first-hand experience is the best converter [S10] and a persona cannot testify (Ground rule 4), a sizeable share of Taiwan 業配 briefs will be unsuitable. The addressable set is brands that accept explanation, comparison and demonstration. How big that set is: not found.

**Compliance**
- The Fair Trade Commission (公平交易委員會, 公平會) treats sponsored posts as endorsement advertising (薦證廣告); material connections must be disclosed; endorsers can share liability; fines for false advertising are reported as NT$50,000 to NT$25 million [S11] (secondary law-firm and press summaries; statute text and article numbers, which I recall as Art. 21 and Art. 42, not seen; see `legal.md`).
- The FTC said in April 2026 that it monitors AI-using ads, has found illegal cases and is weighing mandatory AI disclosure [S12]. Whether it treats an AI persona's statements as an "endorsement" is unresolved (my inference only); it is a `legal.md` question.
- Platform tools do not discharge Taiwan law: the Instagram label can only be added in the app and needs brand permission, TikTok needs the Commercial Content Disclosure toggle, YouTube needs the paid-promotion box [S15] (see platforms.md section 4 for the policy URLs).
- Boosted post equals an ad: set the platform AI-disclosure control or risk the duty that produced Meta's NT$5 million fine [S13][S14].
- Sector rules (food, cosmetics, medical, finance) apply to the sponsor's product claims; decline any brief that needs health, weight-loss, cosmetic-efficacy or investment claims (Ground rule 6).

**Registered business?** In practice yes, because brands pay against an invoice or contract (DECISIONS Q5). Whether a 勞報單 route could work for a studio-run persona before registration: unverified, ask the 記帳士.

### 3.3 LINE stickers (LINE Creators Market 貼圖)

**Verified second-hand**
- Taiwan users send about 60 million stickers a day; LINE messages exceed 1 billion a day [S22]. Brands use stickers as a distribution channel: 7-ELEVEN's OPEN將 has had 8 rounds of LINE sticker themes since 2020 and over 10 million cumulative downloads (company statement, date not shown) [S23]. That shows demand for a *character with distribution*, not what an unknown creator earns.
- The Creators Market terms (clause 8.13, number unconfirmed) say LINE may display that content is AI-generated, triggered by the creator's declaration or LINE's own judgement, and LINE is not liable for damage from the label. The terms do not ban AI-made stickers [S21]. A third-party guide (May 2026) claims AI stickers are auto-flagged and IP infringement is strictly prohibited; low confidence [S21].
- Policy URLs to open: `https://creator.line.me/en/terms/`, `https://creator.line.me/en/review_guideline/`, `https://creator.line.me/en/guideline/` [S21].

**Not verified or not found**
- Current rule on AI-created stickers beyond the label: **not confirmed**. The terms I could see are permissive; the review guideline text was not read.
- Eligibility of Taiwan-based creators, minimum payout, payout schedule, price tiers, sticker count rules: **not found**.
- Revenue share: I recall that creators receive about half of net sales after store fees and taxes [U1]. Unverified; I did not see the live page.
- Creator earnings distribution in Taiwan: **not found**. Do not budget any sticker income.

**Fit and risk**
- Production fit is good: a persona's expressions, outfits and catchphrases map onto a pack, and the batch can be generated and reviewed in one session. The risks are character consistency across the pack, garbled or Simplified Chinese on image, and rejection for similarity to existing IP (Ground rule 2 and the provenance file help here).
- Copyright in AI-generated art: my recollection is that Taiwan's IP office treats output with no human creative input as unprotected [U2]. If true, the studio may not be able to stop copying of a purely AI-generated sticker set. Question for `legal.md`.
- Time to first revenue: 1 to 2 months to list (estimate); the first payout date is unknown.
- **Registered business?** Unverified; Creators Market is built for individuals, but payout, withholding and tax handling for a Taiwan resident is a 記帳士 question.

### 3.4 LINE Official Accounts (官方帳號)

OA is a **channel and a cost, not a revenue stream**. Its roles: retention after VOOM closed on 2026-09-30 [S38], delivery of affiliate links, sticker promotion, B2B lead capture.

Add-on messages from 2026-11-01: NT$0.20 each for the first 50,000 in a month, NT$0.15 beyond [S24]. Figures come from third-party summaries of LINE's notice; confirm at `https://tw.linebiz.com/column/LINEOA-2026-Price-Plan/` [S24].

- **Billing unit unresolved:** whether one broadcast to N friends counts as one message or N messages is not confirmed [S24]. If it is N, the free 200 messages vanish once an OA has more than 200 friends and 中用量 allows about three broadcasts to 1,000 friends a month (arithmetic on the unresolved assumption). Do not size an OA budget until this is checked in OA Manager.
- Automated-reply disclosure: LINE's own rule requiring bot disclosure: not found. BRIEF rule 9 applies: every automated reply says it is automated. LINE's AI reply feature was reported as limited to some Japan users [S26].

- Cost versus budget: one OA at 中用量 is NT$1,000 of the NT$5,000 cap, before the reviewer. Ten OAs are out of the question. Recommendation: stay on 輕用量 until an audience justifies more, and use at most one studio-level OA, not one per persona. Verification requirements for a green or blue "verified" OA: not found.

### 3.7 Producing AI ad creative for brands as a service (B2B)

**What the evidence supports (all inference from verified incidents)**
- Taiwan brands are already using AI imagery and being punished for sloppy or undisclosed use: Life8's AI model that allegedly copied a real model's features and tattoo (July 2026) [S31]; IKEA Taiwan's showroom image with distorted fingers (June 2026) [S32].
- Regulators are acting on ads with AI personal images: Meta was reportedly fined NT$5 million for ads with undisclosed AI-generated personal images, under the Fraud Crime Hazard Prevention Act (詐欺犯罪危害防制條例); the press reports are dated 2026-09-23 and the decision date and article numbers are unverified [S13].
- So a studio that delivers labelled, provenance-documented, native-reviewed AI creative has a compliance-shaped selling point. `competitors.md` reached the same conclusion for fashion: revenue is more likely from B2B creative than from follower-based sponsorship.
- The counter-signal: audiences and brand marketers are sceptical of AI ads [S2][S33][S34], and "AI creators can go viral but can't close the sale" (TechNews, 2026-03-30; page not read, low) [S36]. B2B creative is about product imagery and storyboards for the brand's own accounts, not about the persona's follower trust.

**Prices.** Pricing of comparable Taiwan agencies and freelancers per video or per image: **not found.** Brand budgets per video or image: **not found.** Use section 4's quote-gathering plan; ask the operator's fashion contacts first.

**Requirements**
- Registered business and 統一發票 capability (DECISIONS Q5), a portfolio (the pilot personas' content can serve), a written scope with a fixed number of revision rounds, and a commercial-use licence on every generator used (that is `pipeline/tools.md` territory).
- A native reviewer on every deliverable. The reviewer cost belongs in the job price.
- Clear refusal list: no health, medical, weight-loss or cosmetic-efficacy claims, no financial advice, no gambling, no politics (Ground rule 6). This removes much of the fitness and wealth client base the operator knows; fashion is the open lane.

**Time to first revenue.** 1 to 3 months after registration (estimate), mostly outreach and one or two paid pilot jobs.

**Compliance**
- The client's sector rules govern their ad content; the studio must not produce what the client may not claim.
