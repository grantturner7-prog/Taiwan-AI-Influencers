## 3. Scoring framework

### 3.1 Criteria (each scored 1 to 5 as an integer)

| Code | Criterion | 5 means | 1 means |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Audience size in Taiwan | Mass-market interest across most adult age groups | Very small audience |
| B | Advertiser spend reachable by a compliant account | Heavy digital category spend that a no-claims, no-advice account can access | Little or no sponsor money |
| C | Competition intensity | Saturated with human creators and agencies | Open field |
| D | Fit for an openly virtual character | Value is delivered without lived experience; visual or character-led; precedent exists | Value depends on lived experience, credentials or intimate trust |
| R | Compliance risk under Ground rule 6 and Taiwan sector ad rules | Rule 6 core territory (medical, weight-loss, investment advice, gambling, politics) | No sector regime, no Rule 6 contact |

Risk anchors: 1 no sector regime. 2 minor regime (IP, platform policy, generic endorsement rules). 3 a sector regime applies to some sponsors, or phrasing needs care. 4 high penalties and high temptation to cross Rule 6. 5 Rule 6 core territory.

### 3.2 Evidence tags (shown in section 6.1)

- **S** = the score's driver is directly sourced (a size or share of the right category).
- **P** = proxy: a sourced datum about something related (a growth rate, a single creator's subscribers, an industry's revenue).
- **J** = judgement with stated reasoning, no sourced datum.

Competition (C), fit (D) and risk (R) are judgement by nature; their basis is in section 6.2 and the legal anchors are in section 7.

### 3.3 Weights and formula

Competition and risk are inverted in the formula so a higher total is always better.

**Total = 0.15 x A + 0.15 x B + 0.15 x (6 - C) + 0.25 x D + 0.30 x (6 - R)**  (maximum 5.00, minimum 1.00)

| Term | Weight | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Safety (6 - R) | 30% | Brief section 1 ranks "accounts stay in good standing" first; Ground rule 6 is a stop-and-ask rule [S22] |
| Fit (D) | 25% | Proxy for whether engagement is achievable under Ground rule 4 (no invented experience) and for evidence that audiences accept a character in that niche |
| Audience (A) | 15% | Reach, second priority in the brief (engagement) |
| Advertiser spend (B) | 15% | Revenue, third priority in the brief |
| Openness (6 - C) | 15% | Room to be noticed |

Worked example, FAS1 (fashion): A 4, B 4, C 4, D 4, R 2.
0.15 x 4 + 0.15 x 4 + 0.15 x (6 - 4) + 0.25 x 4 + 0.30 x (6 - 2) = 0.60 + 0.60 + 0.30 + 1.00 + 1.20 = **3.70**.

Treat differences under about 0.25 as ties. Section 10 shows ranks move several places under other weights.

### 3.4 Screens applied before the shortlist

A row can be ranked but not shortlisted unless it passes all of:
1. R is 3 or less. R = 5 is **blocked**. R = 4 is **narrow-only**: it may enter only through a narrower row that scores R of 3 or less.
2. A is 3 or more, and A + B is 6 or more (stops tiny audiences floating up on low risk alone).
3. D is 3 or more (Ground rule 4: the character cannot taste, wear, drive or test).
4. Cannibalisation: one niche per audience cluster, unless the audiences are clearly distinct (section 9.2).
