# Competitors: virtual influencers, VTubers and AI characters in Taiwan, plus regional benchmarks

Phase 1 research file. Written 2026-10-10. This is research, not legal advice.
Scope: virtual influencers (虛擬網紅), VTubers (虛擬實況主), AI-generated characters and brand mascots (吉祥物) that are active in Taiwan, plus the strongest regional examples used only as benchmarks.

Contents: 1 Bottom line, 2 Method and limits, 3 Taiwan landscape, 4 Regional benchmarks, 5 Taiwan trust and backlash cases, 6 Analysis, 7 Does the evidence support demand, 8 Where this complicates the brief, 9 Open questions, then Sources.

---

## 1. Bottom line

1. **Taiwan has a real, monetised VTuber market, but I found no Taiwan-based, openly-AI, photoreal "virtual influencer" account with a verifiable audience.** The Taiwan cases I could verify are anime-style VTubers operated by a human performer (中之人), agency idol projects, brand-owned characters, and one corporate AI-driven character (宮祈緣). [S15][S18][S19][S23]
2. **Most names in the usual "virtual influencer" lists are not Taiwan-based.** Ayayi is mainland Chinese, Ria and Imma are Japanese, Rozy is Korean, Aitana is Spanish, Rui is Korean. I found no Taiwan virtual influencer called "ANNA". [S26][S37][S39][S40][S41][S47]
3. **The first-generation global virtual influencers look plateaued, not collapsing.** Third-party tracker snapshots (single tracker, short windows) show follower counts flat or slightly falling in Aug to Sep 2026 for Imma, Lil Miquela and Rozy (Rozy: about -1.4% over a year); Noonoouri is described only as "slight decline" without a figure. Imma, Rozy and Noonoouri are still above the older counts reported for them, so "stagnant" is a reading of recent windows only. Engagement on sponsored work is low where measured. [S39][S41][S42][S43] (Verifier edit 2026-10-10: softened from "stagnant"; the author's own table shows Rozy at about 160,651 versus 47,000 to 150,000 in older reports.)
4. **The "TVBS" story is a Taiwanese report on a UK Guardian investigation, not a Taiwan brand scandal.** TVBS ran it on 2026-06-23; the Guardian piece is dated 2026-06-21 in secondary coverage. The Taiwan-origin trust incidents in 2026 are of a different kind: AI model likeness (Life8), poor AI imagery (IKEA Taiwan), fake AI "evidence" (ALT), and deepfake fraud ads. [S1][S3][S7][S8][S9][S55]
5. **No Taiwan regulator action on AI personas posing as real customers was found.** The Fair Trade Commission (公平會) said in April 2026 it now monitors AI-using ads, has found illegal cases, and is still weighing whether to mandate AI disclosure. [S4]
6. **Fashion is the best-supported niche for a virtual character. Fitness and wealth are where independent 2026 commentary says AI "lack of authenticity" becomes a liability.** I found no Taiwan virtual or VTuber fitness or personal-finance channel. [S50][S56][S55]
7. **Verdict on demand for DISCLOSED virtual characters in Taiwan: unclear, leaning weak for the product described in the brief.** Demand for VTuber and idol IP is clearly there; demand for an AI influencer account that recommends products is not evidenced. See section 7.

## 2. Method and limits (read this before using any number)

- **Page fetching was blocked in this environment.** WebFetch failed with DNS errors on every host tried, and `curl` through the session proxy returned HTTP 403 on the hosts tested (bnext.com.tw, en.wikipedia.org). Per the proxy README those are policy denials, so I did not try to route around them.
- **All evidence therefore comes from search-engine result summaries** (headlines, snippets, summaries of pages), not from reading platform pages or full articles. **No follower count below was read from a first-party Instagram, YouTube or TikTok page.**
- **"Date observed" in this file means the date of the source report or tracker snapshot**, not a live reading. Third-party trackers (CreatorDB, HypeAuditor, Favikon) estimate counts and compute engagement differently; treat as indicative only. [S39][S41][S42]
- **"not found"** means I searched and no public number appeared in the results. It does not mean the account has no audience.
- Chinese-language keywords searched: 虛擬網紅, 虛擬網紅 台灣, AI網紅, AI 虛擬人, VTuber 台灣, 虛擬偶像, 品牌虛擬代言人, 數位人, 虛擬主播, AI 主播, plus names. Searches for "Ria" and "ANNA" as Taiwan characters returned nothing Taiwan-based.
- Foreign currency is shown in the original currency. I did not convert to NT$ because I have no dated FX source.
- Confidence labels: high, medium, low, unverified, as in the Sources table.

## 6. Analysis

### 6.1 What works (with evidence strength)

| Pattern | Evidence | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Character as owned IP with a fandom infrastructure (agency, music, concerts, merch) | Springfish: 1.8 million combined subscribers (company figure), 280+ collaborations, UMG deal 2026-01-16; Japanese split shows merch outranking ads [S18][S19][S30] | Medium (company-reported) |
| Brand-owned characters that the brand distributes itself | OPEN將 stickers over 10 million downloads; 中華電信's contest-built spokes-character; Lu do Magalu [S25][S27][S44] | Medium |
| Fashion and visual-first categories | Mar 2026 industry commentary: entertainment, fashion, gaming work; health, family, finance, wellness suffer from the authenticity gap [S50] | Low-medium (summary only) |
| Clear, early "this is a character" framing | LBB (2026-03-24) argues Asian audiences accept artifice; Mia Zelu shows what happens when disclosure is buried [S45][S48] | Medium |
| Entertainment/education formats that do not require lived experience | 宮祈緣's 《結緣金》 is a role-play "day in a job" format with AI-generated responses [S23] | Low (no audience data) |
| Large reach on single campaign assets | Two Rozy Shinhan Life commercials, 17.2 million views combined [S41] | Medium |

### 6.2 What fails or stalls

| Pattern | Evidence |
|---|---|

### 6.3 Whitespace by niche

The operator's niches are health-fitness, wealth and fashion. "Whitespace" here means "no Taiwan virtual competitor found". It may be empty because it is hard.

**Fitness**
- Competitors found: **none that are virtual or VTuber.** Human fitness YouTubers dominate: tracker snippets list 營養健身葛格 Peeta at about 800,000 subs, Hot Emma at about 1.7 million (stated as audience), CobraCamp at about 150,000 (Heepsy/CreatorDB snapshots, mid-2026; low-medium) [S56].
- Adjacent signal: 宮祈緣's series includes 健身 as one of about 20 themes, in a "try a job" entertainment format, not coaching [S23].
- Why it may be empty: fitness depends on a trainer's credibility and lived results, which a character cannot claim (Ground rules 4 and 6). The Mar 2026 commentary lists health among sectors where AI authenticity gaps become a liability [S50].
- Candidate angles to test against Ground rules 4 and 6 (the studio must still screen each post; no personal-experience claims, no health, injury-prevention, body-change or weight-loss outcome claims): equipment and gym-etiquette explainers, general exercise-vocabulary or programme-structure explainers, gear comparisons, workout-timer or music formats. "Form diagrams" and "programme templates" are the riskiest of these because they can read as instruction or health advice; treat as unapproved until the operator decides at STOP 1. These compete with human creators on trust, not on novelty.
- Assessment: **open but low-trust; unclear commercial value.**

**Wealth / personal finance**
- Competitors found: **no Taiwan virtual character in personal finance.** Korea's Shinhan Life used Rozy in TV ads for insurance, showing a financial brand will pay for a virtual spokes-character, but with a corporate sponsor [S41].
- Environment: AI-face fraud is prominent in Taiwan investing (TWSE chairman, 簡立忠 deepfakes, August 2026; investment fraud the largest loss share, per police data cited) [S55]. An AI face talking about money starts at a trust deficit.
- Compliance: Ground rule 6 bars investment advice. The only compliant sub-angles are consumer money-literacy (budgeting, reading statements, tool explainers, scam awareness).
- Possible differentiator: a character that openly says "I am AI; never take investment tips from any face online" is on-message with the 2026 climate, but it is a small, safe niche, not a growth engine. This is my hypothesis, not a finding.
- Assessment: **widest gap and weakest evidence for demand; highest reputational risk.**

**Fashion**
- Competitors found in Taiwan: **no disclosed virtual fashion influencer.** Brands are using undisclosed AI models, with a likeness dispute (Life8, July 2026) [S7].
- Global proof: Noonoouri (Dior, Valentino, MiuMiu), Imma, Ria, Aitana show fashion is the most-used niche, but follower growth and engagement are weak in 2026 snapshots [S39][S40][S43].
- Risks specific to Taiwan: portrait right (Civil Code art. 18, per commentary) means a face too close to a real model is a legal exposure; AI generation can add design details a real garment lacks (reported in a mainland China e-commerce piece), so no "wearing it" claims (Ground rule 4) [S32].
- Opportunity: a clearly labelled character presenting outfit logic, colour, fit explainers and brand lookbooks, with revenue more likely from B2B creative (brands needing disclosed AI models) than from follower-based sponsorship. Also from the numbers: first-generation benchmarks suggest follower-based 業配 revenue will be thin.
- Assessment: **best fit with evidence; prefer B2B angle.**

### 6.4 Implications for a 6 hours a week, NT$5,000 a month solo operator

- The only Taiwan operators with scale have a company behind them (Springfish, Gamania, 魔競, So-net, Chunghwa Telecom). The nearest analogue to a studio of ten characters is an agency roster, which in the cases found are staffed businesses. [S16][S18][S22][S23]
- Human-performed VTubers are a bad template: 100 to 200 stream hours a month is the stated agency expectation for some rosters [S16].
- Disclosure must be in the caption and on the image, not only in the bio: Mia Zelu's bio disclosure failed in practice [S45].
- Native zh-TW review on every post is justified by the evidence: IKEA Taiwan and Life8 show Taiwan audiences notice AI mistakes quickly and call them out [S7][S8].

## 7. Does the evidence support demand for DISCLOSED virtual characters in Taiwan?

**Answer: unclear, leaning weak for the product in the brief; clear and monetised for a neighbouring product (VTuber and idol IP).**

Evidence that supports demand:
- Taiwan has about 2,595 counted VTubers (2024) [S28] and a company-stated 1.8 million combined subscribers for one agency (2025) [S18].
- Universal Music chose a Taiwan VTuber agency for its first Chinese-language virtual-idol debut (2026-01-16) [S19].
- Corporates keep commissioning characters: Yahoo TV, Chunghwa Telecom, So-net, Gamania with 霹靂 [S15][S22][S23][S25].
- Taiwan donation data suggest virtual performers take a large share of live-stream payments (single magazine source) [S29].
- A KOL Radar article cites a 2022 survey where 58% follow at least one AI virtual influencer (survey origin unspecified; low confidence) [S59].
- A six-market Southeast Asia survey (N=6,000) found mostly positive views of virtual influencers, but Singapore was the most likely to find them "weird" [S46].

Evidence that weakens or complicates it:
- That demand is for anime-style, personality-driven characters, usually with a human performer and live streaming, sold through fandom, merch and concerts. It is not evidence of demand for a photoreal AI "influencer" who recommends products.
- **I found no Taiwan survey of attitudes to virtual influencers**, and no Taiwan-based AI-native influencer with a verifiable audience. Absence may reflect search limits (section 2), not market reality.
- The one AI-autonomous Taiwan character (宮祈緣) is corporate-backed and has no public audience data in the results. [S23]
- 2026 trust signals point the other way: Guardian/TVBS story, Life8, IKEA Taiwan, Hootsuite as quoted second-hand (one third less likely to buy after AI ads), NielsenIQ (AI ads rated worse), Kolr (verifiable human creators scarce and valued) [S1][S7][S8][S37][S51][S52].
- Benchmark virtual influencers are not growing and sponsored-post engagement is low [S42].
- The Fair Trade Commission may mandate AI disclosure. That could make disclosed characters the norm, or attach stigma to all AI faces; not determinable now. [S4]

What would change this conclusion cheaply (suggested tests, not done):
1. A native speaker spends about 30 minutes on Instagram, Threads, TikTok and Dcard searching Chinese tags for Taiwan-based AI personas and logs their live counts.
2. One pilot persona is tested against a measurable question (saves, shares, link clicks), per the brief's definition of success.
3. Ask two or three Taiwan agencies or brands whether they would pay for disclosed AI creative (the B2B route).

## 8. Where the research complicates the brief or the operator's niches

1. **"Press coverage of brands passing off AI personas as real customers" (brief section 4)**: confirmed as a Taiwan news story, but it is TVBS and UDN relaying a UK Guardian investigation. I found no Taiwan brand named in a fake-customer case and no Taiwan regulator action. Cite it as "foreign investigation reported in Taiwan". [S1][S2][S3]
2. **Most "regional examples" are weaker than their reputations**: Imma, Rozy, Lil Miquela and Noonoouri are flat or slightly down in recent 2026 tracker snapshots (single tracker, short windows) with low engagement where measured. Use them as proof of brand demand, not of audience growth. [S39][S41][S42][S43]
3. **Niches**: fitness and wealth are exactly the sectors independent commentary flags as hard for AI personas, and also the two where Ground rule 6 limits content. Fashion is the best-supported. [S50]
4. **Studio of ten**: the Taiwan precedents for rosters are staffed agencies. A solo operator at 6 hours a week should expect ten accounts to be a portfolio of cheap tests, not ten audiences. [S16][S18]
5. **Likeness**: Taiwan has no virtual-persona law, but portrait-right disputes already hit a Taiwan brand in July 2026. Ground rule 2 (original likeness, provenance file) matches the real risk. [S28][S7]
6. **Disclosure placement**: the brief's "in the bio" is not enough on its own, as Mia Zelu showed; use bio, caption and platform label together. [S45]
7. **Political AI personas** (TFC cases) are in the evidence only as trust context; politics is out of scope. [S12]
