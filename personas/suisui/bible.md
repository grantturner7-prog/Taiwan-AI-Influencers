## 2. Identity

### 2.1 Chinese name: 歲歲

| | |
|---|---|
| Characters | **歲歲** |
| Pinyin | Suìsuì (Hanyu Pinyin). 注音: ㄙㄨㄟˋ ㄙㄨㄟˋ |
| Literal meaning | 歲 (year, age, harvest-year) doubled. 年年歲歲 and 歲歲年年 are familiar phrases for "year after year", so the doubled form reads as "every year" and echoes the festival calendar the persona explains. English gloss: "year by year" |
| Role in the concept | The persona walks through the annual cycle of festivals and customs and cites what named sources say about each (`portfolio.md 2D` row 6) |

**Why it works**

- Two characters, easy to type, say and remember; the doubled form sounds like a friendly nickname rather than a title.
- It states the cycle without a religion, region, nation or authority word (no 老師, 專家, 官方, 文化局, 民俗, 傳統).
- It sits inside the auspicious phrase 歲歲平安, which gives the name a warm, seasonal feel (flags below).
- It pairs with the design: a person who carries blank scrolls and a seal, one new card for each time of year.

**Collision and risk flags (none has been checked; this is not clearance)**

1. **Common nickname and phrase.** 歲歲 is, as `portfolio.md 2A` notes, a common nickname, and the phrase 歲歲平安 is everywhere in greetings; it is probably also used by shops, cafés, stationery, books and accounts (unverified). A TIPO search (Chinese and romanised) and platform searches are needed (HUMAN_TODO 16, `legal.md 8.1`). Likely classes are entertainment, advertising, printed matter and digital content; **class numbers not checked**.
2. **Child-like or cute read.** Doubled names (乖乖, 球球, 糰糰) are often used for children, pets and cute characters in Taiwan (recalled, unverified). For a stated 42-year-old this could pull the adult read down (Ground rule 3). The wife rules on whether 歲歲 reads as a grown-up's nickname or a child's. Mitigation: the adult face, the bio and never using the name in third-person baby-talk (3.2).
3. **碎碎念 sound-alike.** 歲歲 and 碎碎 sound the same, and 碎碎念 means "nagging" or "going on and on". It can be played as a joke (J4, "不碎碎念，一張圖說完"), but the wife must say whether it reads as friendly or as a dig at the audience. 歲歲平安 itself comes from the custom of saying 碎碎平安 when something breaks, which makes the name a blessing pun (recalled, unverified).
4. **Fortune adjacency.** 歲 appears in 太歲 (犯太歲, 安太歲), a fortune and temple-service topic. The name may attract fortune questions. The standing answer is a decline (S15), and fortune content is barred (choice C8).
5. **Greeting-card genre.** 歲歲平安 is a blessing used on 長輩圖 and greeting images. The name may attract that audience and those sponsors; the persona declines blessing-chain content (7.2 item 13).
6. **Mainland-coded read.** The word is used on both sides of the strait and carries no mainland marker by itself. The wife confirms (unverified).
7. **Sound-alikes and look-alikes.** Check 碎碎, 睡睡, 穗穗, 歲歲 with a different final character, and brand names in the classes the lawyer picks (`legal.md 8.1`).
8. **Pinyin handle.** `suisui` is also a plausible pinyin of real given names (for example 穗穗) and a Japanese word (すいすい); a handle that matches a real person's name is a Ground rule 2 collision risk. The check in 2.2 includes searching the handle as a name.
9. **Alternates.** 小滿 (portfolio alternate) is a solar term (about May 21, recalled) and a very common given name and brand word; it fits the calendar theme but is crowded. 巷口 (portfolio alternate, "alley mouth") is plain and local but says nothing about customs. 時節 ("the season, the time of year") is **idea only**, very generic and unchecked. None is checked.
10. **Gendered pronoun.** Copy refers to the character by name or as 「這個角色」, or has it say 「我」; never 他 or 她 (choice C2).

### 2.5 Values

2. No single right way. Regions, families and temples differ, and the cards say so.

4. Quiet on the hard days: nothing flippant on 清明, 中元, 228 or after a disaster.
5. Honesty about what it is: AI, no voice, no family, no home, no attendance.
6. No fear and no luck-selling: no "otherwise bad luck", no lucky colours, no forecasts.
7. Neutral by default: no politics, no identity, no nation words; customs are described by what sources say, not by who "owns" them.

## 4. Visual identity

### 4.1 Concept in one paragraph

歲歲 is a clearly adult, gender-neutral figure in a stylised relief-print (woodblock-style) look: carved outlines that vary in thickness, shadow drawn only as parallel carved hatching, a few flat colour plates, a slightly off-register brick-red plate, and faint paper grain. The face is calm and plain but adult (defined jaw, cheekbones, narrow eyes, straight thick brows, two short carved smile lines), the hair is collar-length and tucked behind one ear with two or three paper-coloured lines at one temple, and the figure carries a **cylindrical canvas scroll-case satchel** with blank rolled scrolls tied with brick-red paper bands. The wardrobe is plain everyday clothing in clay grey, slate brown and canvas. The character appears beside the festival object or scene being explained, usually small in a corner, and every finished card closes with a small brick-red 歲歲 seal. The concept comes from `portfolio.md 2D` row 6; the specifications below extend it. The look is built to stay clear of temples, deities, Japanese woodblock prints, revolutionary-era woodcut posters, Chinese New Year print gods and any existing mascot (4.10, 5.10).

### 4.6 Art medium and production layers

Three layers, produced separately and combined in post:

1. **Character layer.** Relief-print-style art, generated on a plain background from the master references, then cut out. Hand-finished by the operator (outline cleanup, palette equalising, hand check, scroll-band colour) so there is a documented human contribution (`legal.md 8.2`; AI-art copyright is an open lawyer question).
2. **Card layer.** Built by a human in a design tool: the layout, **all** Chinese text, the 「AI 生成」 badge, the studio line, the source footer and the seal. A generator never writes any text (`culture.md 7.2`).
3. **Print-look layer.** The same paper grain and brick-plate offset (about 1 to 2 pixels at 1080 pixels wide; assumption) applied to every final by one saved template or action, so that consistency does not depend on the generator. The offset moves the red plate only, never the outline.

Formats (assumption; check the current limits, unverified): feed image 4:5 portrait (for example 1080 by 1350), square 1:1, Reels 9:16, cover image per the Page's current size, avatar 1:1 shown as a circle (keep the face and the scroll tops inside the circle). Lighting: none; flat. No gradients, glow, lens flare or glossy effects. For a later LINE sticker export a die-cut border is added in post; sticker size and count rules are unverified (`monetisation.md 3.3`).

## 5. Character consistency sheet

### 5.1 Principles

1. Anchor every generation to the approved master references; never to a previous output alone.
2. Generate the character on a plain background and build the cards in a design tool; do not ask a generator for full festival scenes with people, text or numbers.
3. One change per generation (one outfit, one pose, one expression).
4. Text-free output only; all text is added after.
5. Log every asset in `assets/provenance.csv` the same day (5.11).
6. No image-to-image from photos of real people, ever (`portfolio.md 2D`). No prompt names a living artist, studio, brand or existing character, including 虎妮, 宮祈緣, any mascot, any 年畫 or print artist. The avoid-list in 5.4 uses generic descriptions only.
7. Check the hands (thumb plus three fingers) and the scroll case (cylinder, blank scrolls) on every output before anything else.
8. Add the print-look layer (4.6) in post, identically, to every final.

Hex values in prompts are hints; image models follow named colours loosely. Check the output against the palette card (R12) with a colour picker and correct colour in post.

### 9.3 Brief red flags

A brief that asks for any of the following is declined or rewritten:

- 「我拜過」, 「我們家」, 「小時候」, 開箱, 愛用, 心得, 「吃過才推薦」, or any first-person experience.
- Efficacy, luck or fear wording: 開運, 招財, 保佑, 靈驗, 「不買會倒楣」, a "lucky colour of the year", a zodiac forecast.
- The character shown worshipping, praying, visiting a temple or taking part in a ritual, or a deity, altar, incense or joss paper in the picture.
- Calling one custom or one region's way "correct", "authentic" or "the only right way", or ranking regions or families.
- Political, party, campaign, flag, cross-strait or identity content, or any link to an election; "culture exchange" briefs from political bodies.
- A "history" angle that uses era names, rulers, wars, migration stories or national-origin claims.
- Indigenous peoples' patterns, names or imagery.
- Health claims about festival foods or herbs (進補, 養生, 驅蟲), or any medical wording.
- Money amounts or financial framing for 紅包 or gifts.
- Removing, hiding or softening the AI or 業配 disclosure, or posting without the platform tool.
- Use of the institution's or brand's logo as if the character endorsed it, or the character presented as an official mascot, ambassador or authority.
- A real person's or celebrity's face or voice, or a look-alike.
- Persona-to-persona recommendation, or the same brand in the same ISO week as another persona.
- Exclusivity that stops the studio working with other institutions or brands, beyond a narrow, time-limited scope.
- Skipping review (no operator source check, no wife review) to meet a deadline.
- Payment only on sales that would push testimonial-style copy.
