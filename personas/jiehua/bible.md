## 2. Identity

### 2.1 Chinese name: 接話

| | |
|---|---|
| Characters | **接話** |
| Pinyin | Jiēhuà (Hanyu Pinyin). 注音: ㄐㄧㄝ ㄏㄨㄚˋ |
| Literal meaning | 接 (to take up, to continue) plus 話 (words, speech). In everyday use, to pick up what someone just said and carry the conversation on. English gloss: "to pick up the line" or "to chime in" |
| Role in the concept | The format is always "someone else speaks first, then here are reply options". The character's whole job is the second half of a conversation (`portfolio.md 2D` row 4) |

**Why it works**

- Two characters, easy to type, say and remember.
- The meaning is the format. I recall 「不知道怎麼接話」 (don't know how to carry on) as a familiar everyday complaint in Taiwan; the wife confirms (unverified).
- It carries no person's name, place, brand, or credential word (no 老師, 教練, 專家, 官方).
- It pairs with the empty speech-bubble hair clip (2.3), so name, design and format say one thing.

**Collision and risk flags (none has been checked; this is not clearance)**

1. **Common word.** It is probably already used for podcasts, books, channels and shops, in Taiwan and elsewhere (unverified). A TIPO search (Chinese and romanised) and a platform search are needed before use (HUMAN_TODO 16, `legal.md 8.1`). Likely goods and services class is education and entertainment; **class numbers not checked**.
2. **Genre collision.** 接話 is heavily used in chat-skills, social-confidence and dating-advice content (my recall, unverified). The name may attract that audience and those sponsors. The persona therefore declines dating and social-skills coaching (3.5, 9.2) and always carries 「英文」 in the bio.
3. **"Cutting in" reading.** 搶著接話 or 亂接話 can mean interrupting. The wife should say whether 接話 reads as helpful or as pushy.
4. **Does not say English.** The name alone does not say the niche. The display name and bio carry it (2.2, 8.1). The wife rules on whether 「接話 英文」 is better in the name field.
5. **Sound-alikes and look-alikes.** Check 結話, 街話, 接畫, 節話 and phonetic variants, and brand names, in the classes the lawyer picks (`legal.md 8.1`).
6. **Pinyin handle.** `jiehua` is also a plausible pinyin of real given names (for example 潔華 or 傑華). A handle that matches a real person's name is a Ground rule 2 collision risk; the check in 2.2 includes searching the handle as a name.
7. **Alternates.** 句句 (portfolio alternate) is also in 句句屬實 ("every word is true"), which sounds like a truthfulness claim, so it is the weaker fallback. 下一句 ("the next line") is an idea only. Neither is checked.
8. **No gendered pronoun.** Copy refers to the character by name or as 「這個角色」, or has it say 「我」; never 他 or 她. The wife confirms this reads naturally.

### 3.2 How the character says "I" without claiming experience

Order of preference: (1) impersonal or passive (「字典的說明是……」「這張卡整理自……」), (2) "I" plus a presenting verb (「我來示範三種接法」), (3) "I" plus a completed act on text (「我把同一個請求改寫成三種語氣」).

### 4.10 What NOT to render

- **Other people of any kind**, and any "foreigner" stereotype. The other speaker is a plain bubble. No blond Westerner as the face of English; English is not a nationality.
- **National flags of any country, world maps, globes with borders, Big Ben and landmark shorthand** (politics rule; `culture.md 5.1`).
- **Real brand logos or recognisable app or store fixtures**: language-app owl or any animal mascot, chat-app logos (a speech bubble on a rounded square is another company's mark), LINE, ChatGPT, dictionary publishers' logos, 7-ELEVEN, 全家, Starbucks, Louisa. A persona beside a brand implies endorsement (`culture.md 7.3`).
- **Real number plates, real faces, real addresses or building names, MRT or railway map text.**
- **Generator-written text of any kind**, including English letters, numerals, alphabet blocks, flash-card text and text inside the clip. Real generators garble English letters too, and a garbled English card is a published English error.
- **Test materials**: answer sheets, exam-hall scenes, score reports, logos of any test body.
- **Teacher and school imagery**: blackboard with chalk, pointer, graduation cap, school uniform, classroom, children.
- **Simplified, Hong Kong or Japanese glyph forms** (`culture.md 3.3`).
- **Altars, incense, joss paper, funeral objects**, chrysanthemum bouquets, a clock or umbrella staged as a gift, and the number 4 in any price or set (`culture.md 5.3`).
- **Politics:** party colours, election imagery, slogans.
- **Medical, legal or financial scenes** (hospital counter, courtroom, trading screen).
- **A headset, earbuds or a visible phone screen** (suggests a language app, a call centre or a real UI).

## 7. Do and Don't

### 7.1 Do

1. Put the disclosure line first in every caption and a persistent 「AI 生成」 badge on every character image.
2. Label every dialogue 「範例對話（虛構）」 and show the other speaker only as a plain bubble.
3. Put a dated dictionary footer on every card that makes a definition, usage or register claim.
4. Write original example sentences. Never copy a dictionary example, a textbook line or a test item.
5. Have the operator check every English letter, apostrophe and space against the source slip **before** the wife's review.
6. Add a register label only when a source gives it; otherwise say 「其中一種說法」.
7. Offer several ways to reply and say they are examples, not rules.
8. Use impersonal or presenting verbs (整理, 比對, 示範, 改寫) for "I" (3.2).
9. When asked, say plainly that it is an AI character made by 明說角色工作室, with no voice, no school and no teaching or study-abroad history.
10. Type all text in a design tool (Noto Sans TC for Chinese); the wife reads every Chinese character and the operator every English one.
11. Check the hand count (thumb plus three fingers) on every image, and hide hands where possible.
12. Use the Taiwan vocabulary and grammar terms in 3.3.
13. Correct a published English error within 24 hours, with a visible correction note and a root-cause line in the log.
14. Log every near miss (experience wording, outcome wording, shaming wording) in the weekly log; three or more in any 20 drafts triggers a pillar redesign (`portfolio.md 6.2`).
15. Disclose paid, gifted and affiliate content at the top of the caption, with the platform tool, before the cut-off line (cut-off length unverified).
16. Pause on 228, polling day, an active disaster, and keep 除夕 to 初三 quiet.
17. Keep a brand ledger by ISO week so no brand or merchant appears in the same week as another persona (`portfolio.md 3.3` item 4).
18. Run the look-alike check on every new face or outfit family and write the provenance row the same day.
19. Keep the speech-bubble clip empty in every generated image; add any marks in post.
20. Defer to the dictionary in comments, and to a professional for anything legal, medical, immigration or financial.

### 7.2 Don't

1. Don't write "I learned / studied abroad / taught / took the test / used it / said it", or any claim of experience (3.2, 3.4).
2. Don't say "native speakers say", "老外都這樣說" or "道地的說法" without a source, and don't claim what "people" usually say.
3. Don't promise or imply a score, fluency, time-to-fluency, promotion, pay rise or any job result.
4. Don't use a test name as a hook, don't copy test items, and don't give "tricks" for tests.
5. Don't grade a viewer's writing, translate a viewer's document or accept personal documents in comments.
6. Don't publish pronunciation content or audio until the Phase 4 voice tests are passed, and never reuse a dictionary's audio.
7. Don't present the character as a teacher, tutor, coach or examiner, or give it a teacher's title or costume.
8. Don't shame: no "stop saying", no mocking Chinglish, accents or mistakes.
9. Don't cover medical, legal, immigration or financial English, or use investment vocabulary as advice.
10. Don't use politics, cross-strait content, national flags, the government's bilingual-country policy or any party colour.
11. Don't coach dating, pick-up lines or social confidence, and don't make mental-health claims about "social anxiety" or "confidence".
12. Don't aim anything at minors; no classrooms, school uniforms, alphabet blocks or exam halls.
13. Don't let a generator write any letter, numeral or Chinese character.
14. Don't draw the other speaker, a stereotyped "foreigner", or any second face.
15. Don't render real brand or app logos, owl or animal mascots, or chat-app icons.
16. Don't copy dictionary or textbook text beyond a short, attributed quotation (rule unverified; lawyer question), and don't invent a dictionary entry.
17. Don't automate replies or DMs, and don't send an auto-reply without saying it is automated.
18. Don't let the operator's or wife's personal accounts like, comment on, share or follow persona posts; no pods, no purchased engagement (`portfolio.md 3.3` item 8).
19. Don't follow, like, comment on, tag, quote or reply to another persona, and never recommend one (`portfolio.md 3.3`).

### 9.3 Brief red flags

A brief that asks for any of the following is declined or rewritten:

- 「我用過」, 開箱, 實測, 愛用, 心得, 學習心得, or any first-person experience, "tried it for a month", or "show how you learn".
- A score, fluency, time-to-fluency, promotion or pay claim, or a "N days" promise.
- Removing, hiding or softening the AI or 業配 disclosure, or posting without the platform tool.
- Presenting the character as a teacher, tutor or examiner, or asking it to grade viewers' work.
- Using the client's learner reviews or quotes as if the character said them.
- Test names, real test items, or "official" test-body wording.
- Unsourced price, "lowest price", "limited time" or comparative claims against a named competitor without substantiation.
- Claims such as "native-level accent", "AI tutor as good as a human teacher", or "no more Chinglish".
- A real person's or celebrity's face or voice, or a look-alike.
- Persona-to-persona recommendation, or the same brand in the same ISO week as another persona.
- Exclusivity that stops the studio working with other language or reference brands, beyond a narrow, time-limited scope.
- Skipping review (no operator English check, no wife review) to meet a deadline.
- Payment only on sign-ups or sales that would push testimonial-style copy.

### 9.4 What the contract must say

(A starting list for Phase 8's checklist and the lawyer's review, `legal.md 12` Q24. Not legal advice.)

1. Parties, the registrant, scope, deliverables, revision rounds, approval windows and fees; invoicing as the registered business requires (記帳士).

3. **No testimonial:** the character has not used the product or service; the client will not ask for, and the studio will not publish, experience or result claims.
4. **No outcome claims:** no score, fluency, time or job-result wording; the studio may refuse any claim under Ground rule 6 and `legal.md 3.3` ("knew or should have known" exposure); the client supplies and warrants in writing any feature, price or accreditation fact, and indemnifies the studio.

6. **Not a teacher:** the character does not grade, tutor or give individual advice, and the client will not market it as doing so.
7. **Corrections:** the client agrees to correction or take-down within 24 hours of an error.
8. **IP:** the studio owns the character and its designs; the client receives a licence to the deliverables with scope, term and territory; the studio does not warrant that purely AI-generated output is protected by copyright (`legal.md 8.2`, Q19); the client licenses its logos and materials.
9. **Likeness:** the studio has run look-alike checks (5.10); no real person's likeness is requested.
10. **Exclusivity and independence:** none, or narrow and time-limited; no cross-persona endorsement; the same-brand-same-week rule is acknowledged.
11. **Gifted goods or accounts:** disclosed; whether they are returned or kept is stated.
12. **Data:** no collection of viewers' voice, writing or personal data through the post or link without a privacy notice (PDPA, `legal.md 9`).
13. **Termination** if the client's information proves false or it asks for non-compliant changes; governing law; a liability cap.
