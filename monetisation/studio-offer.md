> **OPERATOR ONLY（僅限內部，寄出前刪除）: A1. Where this sits in the plan**
>
> * B2B creative is the route the research ranks first for year 1 because it needs no followers and can use the operator's fashion contacts (`research/monetisation.md` section 5; `strategy/portfolio.md` 2C and 6.4). Its income belongs to the **studio**; a character is the showroom, tagged for tracking only.
> * It is **unproven**. Phase 1 found no Taiwan price, demand figure or buyer for this service (`research/monetisation.md` 3.7, gap G11). The counter-signals are real: Kolr (iKala) 2026 says brands now prize verifiable human creators; AI-made ads rate worse with some consumers (NielsenIQ 2024, a vendor study); AI errors get mocked quickly (IKEA Taiwan, 2026-06) and a resemblance claim alone forced an apology (Life8, 2026-07) (all from `research/SUMMARY.md` section i and `research/monetisation.md` S2, S31, S32, S33, medium confidence, not re-checked; these companies are named as evidence sources only and are not outreach targets here: verify existence and current contact route before any outreach, and do not quote the incidents to a brand). The one-sentence answer to a sceptical contact is in Part B section 1: some brands should choose verifiable humans.
> * The demand signal the offer leans on is **inference**, not data: brands are being embarrassed by sloppy or undisclosed AI imagery, and a studio that delivers labelled, source-dated, human-checked work may be useful to them. Do not state it to a brand as a fact.
> * The offer sells **creative, not reach**. A character with no audience has no reach to sell (`monetisation/rate-card.md` tier T0).
> * Capacity: client jobs compete with the characters for the same hours, revenue work is cut before posting (`strategy/portfolio.md` 5.6), and the rate card assumes about 1.5 operator hours a week per client job. **Proposal (not a decision): take one client job at a time until real minutes have been logged.** Rush delivery is not offered.
